The stake

A warm anomaly is a silent inventory write-off.

Shrimp and fish are farmed in open estuarine ponds. When sea-surface temperature spikes or heavy rain crashes the salinity, dissolved oxygen collapses and disease pressure jumps. A single bad event can wipe a grow-out cycle.

Today the farm learns about the anomaly from the pond sensor — at which point the only options left are emergency aeration or an early, loss-making harvest. The information arrives with zero warning time.

The shift

From detection to forecast-then-detect.

Incumbent · detection

Run a detector on data that already exists

Needs the observation y(t) to have happened. By definition it can only flag an anomaly that is already occurring. Warning time: none.

Ours · prediction

Run the same detector on a forecast trajectory

The quantum reservoir rolls the ocean state H steps forward; the detector fires on the predicted series. Warning time: up to the forecast horizon.

Same detection science the field already trusts — moved from "what just happened" to "what is about to."

How it works

One pipeline, from buoy to alarm.

Reservoir rollout
An 8-qubit reservoir feeds its own prediction back, K stochastic samples forward

Residual, matrix-profile & isolation-forest run on each sampled future
Decide
Alarm + ETA + driver
P(anomaly at t+h), how many days out, and which driver is loading

The quantum reservoir is a fixed dynamical system — no training of the reservoir itself, only a closed-form linear readout on top. That makes each forecast cheap, deterministic, and reproducible: the same input gives the same alarm, every time. For a system that has to be trusted operationally, that determinism is a feature, not an accident.

The classical baseline is not a straw man: it is a reservoir of the same feature count, tuned on the same data. On this daily series it also becomes unstable across seeds — one draw blows up to NMSE 1.83. The quantum reservoir's determinism removes that failure mode entirely.

Why you can trust the number

Every claim ships with the baseline
that could have beaten it.

Quantum ML is full of results that evaporate under a fair classical control. Ours is built the opposite way: persistence and a size-matched classical reservoir are on every chart, and when a baseline wins, the code is written to say so by default.

On monthly ENSO data, the classical baseline is at parity or ahead — and we report that. On daily ocean temperature — the cadence an operation actually acts on — the quantum reservoir is the more reliable model at every seed. We tell you exactly where the edge is and where it isn't.

No hidden leakage

Scalers and thresholds are fit on the training span only, structurally — not by discipline.

Calibrated, not point

K sampled futures give a probability whose coverage is measured, so an 80% alarm means 80%.

Cost on the same slide as accuracy

A forecast that needs a supercomputer to match a laptop has answered the business question — so we track it.

The product

An early-warning layer for the farm's operating calendar.

What the operator sees

Per-site, per-week: probability of a heat or salinity anomaly, the estimated days-to-onset, and which driver is loading — heat, ENSO, or rain.

What it plugs into

Aeration scheduling, feed and stocking decisions, harvest timing, and insurance triggers — the choices that are cheap to make early and expensive to make late.
