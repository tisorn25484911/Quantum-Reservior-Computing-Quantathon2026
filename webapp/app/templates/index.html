{# ------------------------------------------------------------- the problem #}

The problem

Southeast Asia grows the food — and takes the drought.

The region produces about a third of the world's rice, feeding ~557 million people. Drought is its costliest weather, and the driver is accelerating: strong El Niño events went from one in 1901–1960 to four in 1961–2020, arriving closer together.

⚠

A plan with no forecast

A rainfall or heat deficit is confirmed only once crops are already dying. National outlooks rarely become farm-level lead time.

🌬

One dry season ends a crop

El Niño years bring ~17% less rain and ~9% lower rice yields. A single failed monsoon wipes a season's feed supply and income.

⏱

Prepare cheap, or pay late

Storing water, tougher seed, shifting planting are all cheap — if you have a head start. Waiting means the harvest is already gone.

Introducing

PronoiaQ — the reservoir that sees the anomaly coming.

We drive an environmental series through an exactly-simulated quantum reservoir, read out a calibrated forecast, then run anomaly detectors on the forecast — so an event is flagged before it happens, with warning time built in.
