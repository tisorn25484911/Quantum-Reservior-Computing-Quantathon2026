Free-running forecast — the honest test

Train on the train span only, then start at the train/test boundary and recurse across the entire test span, feeding the model its own output back into the reservoir at every step — never seeing a single test value. Only then compare the self-generated trajectory to the test set.

Why this is stricter than the Forecast page. The Forecast page re-anchors to real data every step: it predicts one step, then gets the real next value before predicting again. This page does not — once it crosses the boundary it is on its own, which is what “predict the future you don’t have” actually means.
This is designed to be unflattering. A closed-loop rollout of a mean read-out tends to decay toward a flat line, so the honest competitors are on the same axis: persistence (repeat the last value), the seasonal average (replay the per-phase training mean), and a size-matched ESN. If a trivial baseline wins, the verdict says so.

Closed-loop robustness trick (MG task). 0 = off.
Reservoir choice. ising (disordered, fully-connected) is the accuracy default: per Fujii–Nakajima the integrable 1D-chain xxz_hx has structurally poor nonlinear capacity, which is why it loses to ising on daily SST.
Honest note on the paper levers. Virtual nodes and training noise provably raise the reservoir's capacity (see capacity_vs_V.py: nonlinear capacity grows ~4× from V=1→25). But on this blind multi-year free-run they wash out within seed noise — the task is limited by intrinsic predictability, not reservoir capacity, and the seasonal-average baseline beats every setting. So training noise ships OFF by default; turn the knobs to explore, not to expect a better number here.
